# Source and Channel Policies

Design a source plan before searching.

| Topic type | Required or preferred materials | Priority channels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mature academic field | Textbooks, review papers, university courses, standards | Library catalogs, academic indexes, university pages, scholarly societies |
| Fast-moving technology | Recent papers, technical notes, code, benchmarks | Preprint servers, proceedings, official repositories, lab pages, expert newsletters |
| Historical event | Primary documents, archives, oral histories, monographs | National and local archives, libraries, museums, oral-history collections, academic indexes |
| Person or organization | Interviews, speeches, records, biographies, reporting | Official sites, institutional archives, interview collections, reputable press databases |
| Industry | Statistics, regulation, filings, reports, expert analysis | Regulators, statistical portals, company filings, trade associations, specialist publications |
| Practical skill | Documentation, demonstrations, courses, projects | Official docs, reputable course platforms, project repositories, practitioner communities |
| Social issue | Official data, research syntheses, policy, stakeholder views | Public agencies, systematic-review databases, NGOs with transparent methods, academic centers |
| Current event | Primary statements, records, timelines, reporting | Government and institutional sites, public records, reputable newswires, live data |

## Source-plan fields

State:

- topic classification;
- required evidence and resource types;
- channels searched first and why;
- primary/secondary balance;
- recency cutoff or freshness rule;
- languages and geographic scope;
- budget and access limits;
- channels excluded or treated cautiously;
- known evidence gaps.

## Channel selection rules

- Search at least two independent channel families for important claims.
- Prefer canonical pages over aggregators.
- Prefer official repositories over mirrors.
- Use social accounts as update channels, not automatic proof of authority.
- Use community consensus as discovery evidence, not final verification.
- For contested topics, include credible competing interpretations and separate them from primary evidence.
- For fast-moving topics, verify that repositories, courses, and accounts remain active.
- For mature topics, do not over-weight novelty at the expense of stable foundations.

## Coverage stop rule

Stop discovering when the selected set:

1. covers every major branch needed for the user’s goal;
2. includes appropriate primary or authoritative evidence;
3. contains a usable starting resource and practice path;
4. adds little value from the next candidate;
5. fits the user’s time and access limits.
