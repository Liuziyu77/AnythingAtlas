# Topic Taxonomy

Classify a topic before choosing sources.

## Primary types

| Type | Typical need | Credibility emphasis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mature academic field | Coherent foundations and canonical frameworks | Textbooks, reviews, university teaching, scholarly consensus |
| Fast-moving technology | Current concepts, implementations, and active debates | Recent papers, official repositories, conference material, active experts |
| Historical event | Chronology, primary evidence, and competing interpretations | Archives, primary documents, museums, oral histories, peer-reviewed scholarship |
| Person or organization | Reliable biography, statements, decisions, and context | Primary interviews, institutional records, biographies, reputable reporting |
| Industry | Structure, metrics, regulation, and practice | Official statistics, filings, regulators, research reports, domain experts |
| Practical skill | Demonstration, guided practice, and feedback | Official documentation, structured courses, projects, practitioner communities |
| Social issue | Definitions, evidence, stakeholders, and disagreements | Official data, systematic research, policy documents, multiple perspectives |
| Current event | Verified sequence and evolving evidence | Primary statements, public records, reputable reporting, live data |

Use a hybrid classification when necessary.

## Secondary dimensions

Assess:

- **Maturity:** established, developing, or emerging.
- **Rate of change:** slow, moderate, or fast.
- **Evidence burden:** explanatory, academic, professional, or high-stakes.
- **Controversy:** low, interpretive, polarized, or actively disputed.
- **Scope:** global, regional, national, or local.
- **Mode:** theoretical, practical, or mixed.
- **Access:** public, subscription, institutional, or restricted.
- **User mode:** orient, decide, do, troubleshoot, monitor, or study.
- **Urgency:** immediate, near-term, scheduled, or open-ended.
- **Consumption pattern:** short-session, long-form, hands-on, reference, or mixed.

## Knowledge-map checklist

Map:

1. prerequisites;
2. foundational concepts;
3. major branches and dependencies;
4. representative questions;
5. people and institutions;
6. common misconceptions;
7. deeper or adjacent branches.

Also build a user-facing field guide. A knowledge map explains how concepts
depend on one another; a field guide explains what concrete choices, actors,
tools, works, products, methods, or segments the user will encounter now.
Include representative examples and selection rules rather than category names
alone.

Do not force every topic into an academic-discipline structure. For an event,
emphasize chronology and evidence; for a skill, emphasize progression,
practice, and the actual toolchain; for a person, emphasize life phases, works,
context, and primary records; for an industry, include segments, representative
players, products or services, metrics, and selection trade-offs; for a
decision request, lead with the options and conditions that alter the choice.
